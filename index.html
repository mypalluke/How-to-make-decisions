The situation is the same today. We contrast Jehovah’s invitation with the many "streams" of advice—from social media, coworkers, or even our own hearts. Imagine being with an experienced survivalist guide on a hike in the woods. Wouldn’t you ask for his help as you are walking along?

With Jehovah’s help, we can make wise decisions in every aspect of our life. (pause one beat)

II: JEHOVAH HAS EQUIPPED US TO MAKE GOOD DECISIONS (6 min)

To have good clean water, you need the right tools: A filtration system, the manual to know how to use it, and a test kit to ensure the water is safe.

To make good decisions, Jehovah has equipped us with the Bible, thinking ability, and a conscience that when trained by bible principles can test out decisions instantly.

Jehovah doesn’t just drop off these tools to us and hope for the best, notice his interest in each and everyone of us at Isaiah 48:17

17This is what Jehovah says, your Repurchaser, the Holy One of Israel: “I, Jehovah, am your God, The One teaching you to benefit yourself, The One guiding you in the way you should walk. (pause 3 beats)

Jehovah identifies himself as someone that truly cares about us. He is our teacher, he wants us to benefit ourselves. We have the manual to life at our disposal: The Bible.

He not only gives us the manual and leaves it to us to figure it out, he is willing to teach us how to benefit ourselves

If you bought high-end machinery for filtering water, the first thing you’d do is check the manual. Why? Because if you just "wing it," you won't get the results they promised.

Jehovah doesn't want us to just "glance" at the Bible when we're in trouble; waiting to consult the manual until after someone gets sick is too late.

So we read God’s Word regularly - and we ponder over it. (pause 2 beats)

In a world that prizes "life experience," the Psalmist made a bold claim. Let’s look at his secret in Psalm 119:97 and 100:

"How I do love your law! I ponder over it all day long... I act with more understanding than older men, Because I observe your orders."

He had more understanding because he didn't just know the orders—he pondered them. What does it mean to ponder over something? It’s a bit like reverse engineering. It’s taking something apart and seeing how and why it works.

Let’s use a simple example Jesus gave us at Matthew 5:21-23. The Law said: "You must not murder." But is there a direct law that says we should not speak harshly to a neighbor? No.

Yet, Jesus lifted the cover off God’s commands. Murder begins with hatred, so even contemptuous speech matters. Actually, even our thoughts matter. By identifying the principle, we know how to handle situations that are not covered by a direct law. When we ponder over Gods laws it helps us to stop problems where the first malfunction starts. We see the principles. We learn about Jehovah and who he really is.

If we take this into our hearts, it changes who we are down to our core.

In the bible we are also given “case studies”—examples of faithful ones that followed Gods principles and also examples of unfaithful ones that ignored God’s laws and principles. We see how things turned out for them. (1 Cor. 10:11)

So we read Gods word, we ponder over it. What happens? Our conscience that Jehovah has given us is trained. This acts as a sensor to recognize when something is wrong.

Even the best water filtration system won't work if the sensors aren't calibrated. Our conscience is that "hardware." Hebrews 5:14 explains this perfectly, But solid food belongs to mature people, to those who through use have their powers of discernment trained to distinguish both right and wrong.”

When we read the scriptures daily and meditate on their meaning, we use them as a calibration tool for our conscience.

Transition: We realize we are equipped with these powerful tools. Now we just need to put them into action.

III. FOLLOW STEPS THAT LEAD TO A GOOD DECISION (10 min.)

Water filtration process is a 4 stage process: Sedimentary trap, uv light, high pressure filtration, final analysis.

The four steps to making a good decision: patience, prayer, research, analyze motives.

To see how this works, let's weave a common scenario through these four steps. Imagine you are offered a major job promotion. It pays significantly more, but it will require longer hours and some weekend travel. How do we filter this decision?

So what’s the first step in our filter?

Step 1: based on Pr 21:5 “The plans of the diligent surely lead to success, But all who are hasty surely head for poverty.’ Don’t be hasty (stop! think!). When you’re standing in front of a stream and you’re thirsty, the pressure to just 'drink now' is intense. When offered that promotion, the pressure to just say 'yes' for the money is intense. Life is the same. In a physical water filter, the first stage is often a sediment trap. It relies on gravity and time. If you try to force muddy water through a fine filter immediately, you’ll clog the whole system. Spiritually, our "mud" is often our raw emotions— (pause one beat) panic, pride, or the pressure to act now.

Read Deuteronomy 32:29

29If only they were wise! They would ponder over this. They would think about their outcome. (pause 3 beats)

By being patient, we let the "mud" of our excitement or panic settle so we can actually see the situation for what it is. This is not a wait and see attitude, it is diligently pondering the situation.

Stopping and thinking helps you remember you may not have all the answers. So now with clarity you remember then to pray. Youve thought things through. You now have the Clarity of mind to even know what to pray for.

Step 2: Pray for wisdom. In water filtration Once the water looks clear, we use a UV Light. UV light doesn't catch dirt; it disrupts the DNA of invisible bacteria so they can't reproduce. This cannot be effective when the water is cloudy.

James 1:5: "So if any one of you is lacking in wisdom, let him keep asking God, for he gives generously..."

When we pray as James 1:5 suggests, we are asking Him to neutralize the invisible "spiritual viruses"—those subtle worldly philosophies. Apply this to the promotion. We ask Jehovah to neutralize the worldly idea that more money always equals true security. Prayer "sanitizes" our thinking at a level we can't see with the naked eye.

Step 3: Do thorough research. In water filtration, This is the stage of high-pressure filtration. It forces the water through a fine membrane so microscopic that only the pure water molecules can pass.

And what is the result of our thorough research?

Proverbs 2:4,5: "If you keep seeking for it as for silver, And you keep searching for it as for hidden treasures; Then you will understand the fear of Jehovah..."

We are putting our thoughts under the "pressure" of God’s Word. This separates human thinking from Jehovah’s wisdom. For the job promotion, this means researching principles about keeping a simple eye, meeting attendance, and family time. We must carry our own load and avoid asking others “what would you do?”

Step 4: Analyze your motives.

Finally, the water has passed through the machine. But before you take a drink, you pour a little into a small glass for a final inspection. How does it look, smell and finally taste?

Our motives are deeply personal. Sometimes no one can know them outside of ourselves, and of course Jehovah.

Pr 16:2 says: "All of a man’s ways seem right to him, But Jehovah examines the motives."

We can sometimes trick ourselves into thinking after we’ve completed the first three steps that our decision is good. But do our motives truly align with Jehovah’s wisdom?

It’s important to examine ourselves and really test if our decision is sincere. For the job promotion: Are you really taking it to provide for basic needs, or is it for prestige, status, or materialism?

Many times if have proper motives, it will help us to stick to a good decision.

We can also apply this to spiritual goals. Consider a young brother who decides to become a Regular Pioneer. After a while, he’s struggling. When he looks into that small glass, he realizes the "taste" is off. The problem? he was primarily motivated by a desire to please his parents or others.

But notice how running that decision through the filter machine again benefits him. He goes back to Stage 1. Before stopping pioneering, He prays for wisdom. He does thorough research. Finally, he looks at his motives again. This time, he is renewed with vigor and a genuine love for Jehovah and a desire to do his will.

One word of caution for this step four, we might second guess ourselves and keep ruminating over the decision over and over creating a feedback loop. This is not good. If you don’t decide, a decision will be made for you. Oftentimes, it will not be the desired path you would like to go down. Not making the decision is the same as giving away your free will to others for them to use how they want.

In review…

The four step process: Patience, Prayer, Research, Analyze motives.

IMPLEMENT YOUR DECISION (4 min.)

A purified decision is useless if you never “drink the water”.

Do you know how long you'd survive if you don’t take in any water?

Depending on conditions, you could perish in as little as three days!

We can get stuck in a "Feedback Loop" of second-guessing. Paul encouraged the Corinthians to "complete what you started" (2 Cor 8:10, 11).

We are not without help, let’s read (Phil 2:13) says ‘For God is the one who for the sake of his good pleasure energizes you, giving you both the desire and the power to act.’

Jehovah gives us the "energy to lift the glass."

This doesn't mean the path will be easy. Even good decisions can lead to hardships. Think of our brothers in countries where simply serving Jehovah can land someone in prison. Was that a bad decision? Of course not! It was the best decision possible, even if it led to a "hardship."

However, what if we make a decision and then find out we simply cannot fulfill the role? We must be realistic. As Ecclesiastes 3:6 says, there is "A time to search and a time to give up as lost; A time to keep and a time to throw away." If we come to a dead end, we don't just "give up" blindly. We go through the same four-step process again, and that new decision can be a good one, too.
